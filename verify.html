Forecast verification: Alenuihāhā Channel

How the wind forecasts for the Alenuihāhā Channel zone (PHZ121) compared with what the ASCAT satellites measured, pass by pass, for the last 14 days. Built Sat 26 Sep 06:42 HST.

14 passes covered at least 60% of the zone and are scored. Each forecast is compared with the average wind ASCAT measured over the part of the zone it saw, with the models sampled at the same cells.
How it works

ASCAT is a radar on the MetOp-B and MetOp-C satellites that measures the wind at 10 m over the sea from how rough the surface is. It crosses the channel about twice a day, around 09–10 and 20–22 HST, in 12.5 km cells (KNMI's coastal product). Cells flagged for rain or failed quality control are left out. It is the only measurement that covers the whole channel at once, so it is the best ground truth there is out here, with limits: it sees one moment, not an hourly average; cells within a few km of the coast are missing; heavy rain spoils it; and it tends to read a little low in very strong winds.

The forecasts. For each pass, every forecast made for that hour that can still be found, at every lead time: the NWS zone forecast for PHZ121 (every issuance, from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive), NBM (runs 1 to 72 h before, from NOAA's archive on AWS), and RRFS, REFS (ensemble mean) and UH WRF from the runs this briefing downloaded (kept 5 days, so a model is missing when no briefing was built in time). The NWS grid (the forecast grid the zone text is written from, averaged over the zone) is saved at each build. Models are read at the pass's own cells and averaged, so a pass that misses part of the zone compares like with like; their hour is the one nearest the pass.

The zone text gives one range for a whole zone that also has to cover its windiest corner, so it is checked against the middle of what ASCAT saw (the median): within the range, above it or below it. “To 30 kt” with no lower number is read as 20–30.

The scores are grouped by how far ahead the forecast was made. Bias is forecast minus ASCAT, on average (red: the forecast ran high; blue: low); ± is the mean absolute error; the last number is how many passes. A few passes make a noisy score: read them as a trend that firms up over the weeks.
