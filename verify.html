What ASCAT can and can't tell us: read this first (twice a day, never mid-day; not always over the channel; ~2–3 kt error of its own)

When it looks. Only around 09–10 and 20–22 HST, fixed by the MetOp satellites' orbits. It never samples midday or the afternoon, when the trades and the channel jet are often strongest, or the small hours. MetOp-B and -C often pass within an hour of each other, so a pair is two nearly identical looks, not two independent ones.

Where it looks. Each pass covers two 550 km strips with a wide gap between them under the satellite, so many passes miss the channel or see only part of it; passes that see less than 60% of the zone are shown but not scored, and some days have no usable pass at all. A zone average from a partial pass describes only the part it saw (the models are sampled at the same cells so the comparison stays fair).

Near the coast. The coastal product reaches closer to land than the standard one, but cells within a few km of the shore are still missing or contaminated by land in the radar footprint. That is where the channel's edges are, and where the acceleration off Upolu Point and along the Maui coast is sharpest.

What it measures. Radar backscatter from wind-roughened water, turned into a wind at 10 m: strictly an “equivalent neutral” wind (as if the air were neutrally stable) relative to the moving sea surface, so strong currents shift it slightly. Each value is an average over about 12.5 km at a single instant, where a model gives an hourly value on a 2.5–6 km grid. It cannot measure gusts.

What makes it less reliable. Rain and convection (flagged cells are removed, but squalls can still distort cells nearby); very light winds, below about 6 kt, where the signal is weak and the direction uncertain; very strong winds, where C-band scatterometers such as ASCAT tend to read low (above roughly 35–40 kt); and large wind differences inside one cell, as at the jet's edges.

How accurate it is. Against buoys, ASCAT's own error is typically about 2–3 kt in speed and 15–20° in direction in moderate winds, with little bias at the channel's usual speeds and a slight low bias in strong winds. So differences between a forecast and ASCAT smaller than about 2–3 kt are within ASCAT's own uncertainty: read small biases as tendencies, and trust the large, repeated ones.

How to read the numbers.
Bias
The average of forecast minus ASCAT, keeping the sign: whether a source tends to run high (+, it forecast more wind than ASCAT measured) or low (−, less wind than measured, the dangerous side).
± Typical error (mean absolute error, MAE)
The average size of the miss, ignoring whether it was high or low. Two misses of +3 and −1 kt give a bias of +1 kt but an MAE of 2 kt: highs and lows cancel in the bias, not in the MAE. A source can have a small bias and still a large MAE.
n
How many satellite passes were scored.
In range
ASCAT's zone median fell inside the NWS zone forecast's range (e.g. 20–25 kt).
90% within ±X kt
The source's miss was X kt or less on 90% of the passes.
Median, 90th percentile (P90)
Of the ASCAT cells in one pass: the value half the cells were at or below, and the value 90% of the cells were at or below.
Typical error per cell (RMSE)
Root-mean-square difference between forecast and ASCAT across the cells of a pass: like MAE, but weighting the larger misses more.
Pattern match
The correlation between forecast and ASCAT across the cells: 1 = the stronger and weaker wind in exactly the right places, 0 = no relation.
What is compared to what

What ASCAT gives for each 12.5 km cell: its position and time, the wind speed (a 10 m equivalent-neutral wind, in m/s, converted to kt), the direction (ASCAT gives the direction the wind blows toward; it is shown as where it comes from), and quality flags. Cells with no wind or with a rejecting flag (rain detected, wind inversion not successful, variational quality control failed, KNMI quality control failed, noise in any beam above threshold, or not enough good backscatter for a wind retrieval) are dropped; flags that only inform, such as part of the cell over land or ice, do not drop a cell. For each pass the good cells inside the zone (usually 60–70 on a full pass) are kept, and summarised as their mean (zone average), median (the middle cell value), 90th percentile (P90: 90% of the cells were at or below it) and maximum.

The zone forecast is judged on the median because one range must describe the whole zone's typical wind, and the median is not pulled around by a few edge cells; its top is judged on P90 because the forecast also has to cover the zone's windiest part. The models are judged on the mean because their values are averaged over exactly the same cells.

Methods

Data and quality control

The archive runs from 2023-07-01 to 2026-10-10 (UTC) in these periods: Jul–Aug 2023 · Jan–Feb 2024 · Apr–Jun 2024 · Sep 2025 – Oct 2026. Gaps are months the backfill did not cover; within a period every pass found was kept.

Cells. Across the 1,007 passes, 53,816 ASCAT cell positions fell inside the zone; 51,644 were kept and 4.0% removed, either because no wind was retrieved (mostly cells within a few km of land) or because they carried a rejecting KNMI quality flag: rain detected, wind inversion not successful, variational quality control failed, KNMI quality control failed, noise in any beam above threshold, or not enough good backscatter for a wind retrieval. Flags that only inform (part of the cell over land or ice, wind under 3 or over 30 m/s, monitoring) do not reject a cell. Per pass, the median share removed was 2.9%; 43 passes lost more than 10% and the most any pass lost was 50.0%. Nothing else was removed: no outlier screening of the winds themselves.

Flag by flag (recorded from 2026-10-01, 20 passes, 1,017 zone cells; a cell can carry several flags): no wind retrieved 18; variational quality control fails 13; knmi quality control fails 7; any beam noise content above threshold 5; wind inversion not successful 2.

ASCAT is a radar on the MetOp-B and MetOp-C satellites that measures the wind at 10 m over the sea from how rough the surface is, in 12.5 km cells (KNMI's coastal product, from NASA PO.DAAC). It is the only measurement that covers the whole channel at once, so it is the best ground truth there is out here; its limits are set out at the top of the page. Cells with no wind retrieved or with a rejecting quality flag (rain detected, wind inversion not successful, variational quality control failed, KNMI quality control failed, noise in any beam above threshold, or not enough good backscatter for a wind retrieval) are dropped; counts are under data and quality control.

The forecasts. For each pass, every forecast made for that hour that can still be found, at every lead time: the NWS zone forecast for PHZ121 (every issuance, from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive), NBM (runs 1 to 72 h before, from NOAA's archive on AWS), and RRFS, REFS (ensemble mean) and UH WRF from the runs this briefing downloaded (kept 5 days, so a model is missing when no briefing was built in time). The NWS grid (the forecast grid the zone text is written from) is saved at each build.

Sampling. Each model is averaged over every ASCAT cell's 12.5 km footprint (the model points inside it; the nearest point for a coarser grid) at the forecast hour nearest the pass, then over the same cells ASCAT saw, so a pass that misses part of the zone compares like with like. The cell-by-cell scores, maps and area tables use those per-cell values directly.

The zone text gives one range for a whole zone that also has to cover its windiest corner. Its range is checked against ASCAT's median (in, above or below); its top against the 90th percentile; and the middle of the range is scored like a model against the median. “To 30 kt” with no lower number is read as 25–30, as the briefing reads it everywhere, and a single number (“20 kt”) as 17.5–22.5 kt, a 5 kt band like the ranges NWS writes.

The archive. Every pass is kept, with ASCAT's cells and each forecast's value at every cell, so new analyses need no new downloads. Passes from before the briefing ran were added from the public archives (verify_backfill.py): ASCAT from NASA PO.DAAC, the zone forecasts from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet, NBM from NOAA's AWS archive, UH WRF from PacIOOS (its archive keeps only each run's first hours, so those are short leads), and RRFS from NOAA's retrospective runs of RRFS v1 (July 2023, January–February 2024 and May 2024 only). REFS, live RRFS and the NWS grid have no public archive, so they are scored only from the runs this briefing kept. The inversion for each pass comes from the Hilo sounding before it.

Lead time. Lead is measured from each model run's start time (for the zone forecast and the NWS grid, from when they were issued). “Up to a day ahead”, used by every summary on the page, means one run per source and pass: the one made nearest 12 h before the pass, within 0–24 h, never an average of runs (averaging smooths the errors, and the sources keep different numbers of runs). The lead windows use the same rule within each window. The sources' archives differ: NBM keeps runs 1–72 h ahead for every pass; the UH WRF archive has one run a day (06Z) and the RRFS retrospective one (18Z), so their leads are tied to the time of day; the page notes each source's typical lead and its share of evening passes, and splits lead by time of day.

Grouping. Groups smaller than 8 passes show only their count; sources with fewer than 30 passes are flagged as early signs. A few passes make a noisy score: read those as a trend that firms up over the weeks.
