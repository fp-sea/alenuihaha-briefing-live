.SYNOPSIS...
A high pressure system will linger north of the Hawaiian Islands
producing moderate to breezy trades through the middle of next 
week with minor fluctuations in speed. Brief pulses of enhanced 
showers will roll through on the trades during this time period, 
with showers favoring windward mountain areas in the overnight to
early morning hours. 

&&

.DISCUSSION...
This mornings satellite imagery shows the first of three easterly
waves continues to slowly drift through the islands enhancing
shower activity through the early morning hours. Two additional
weak atmospheric waves will drift from east to west across the 
islands on Saturday evening, and from late Sunday into Monday.
Cloud and shower coverage will increase with each passing wave,
favoring the overnight to early morning hours with the highest
rainfall amounts falling over the windward mountain areas of each
island. A wet trade wind pattern for sure with pulses of showers
affecting each island through Monday. 

A brief drying trend will develop by Tuesday as the last of the 
easterly waves passes to the west of the state. Subsidence aloft 
will lower temperature inversion heights with more stable trends 
reducing clouds and shower activity lasting through Wednesday. 
Expect the typical brief passing showers during the overnight 
hours over the windward mountain areas.

The long range forecast remains fairly uncertain as global models
continue to struggle in finding a consistent solution with a
passing short wave mid latitude trough passing just north of the 
Hawaiian Islands. The model solutions have flipped over the past 
few days with the American (GFS) model now showing a drier high 
pressure ridge setting up north of the island chain, and the 
European (ECMWF) model showing a cold core low, cut off from the 
mid latitude westerly winds, deepening just north of the state. 
The ECMWF solution would bring southeasterly to southerly winds 
over the islands, drawing up unstable tropical moisture from the 
deep tropics and triggering a period of wet weather across the 
islands. While the American GFS model shows more typical easterly 
trade wind weather with brief passing shower activity. 
Unfortunately these large scale differences are impossible to 
blend and we will have to wait a bit longer for our global weather
model solutions to converge on a more consistent solution. Stay 
tuned for changes to the long range forecast.

.AVIATION...
Moderate to breezy trades through the period. Low cigs and SHRA 
should impact windward and mauka locations with some limited 
spillover to leeward areas. MVFR conds possible in SHRA, 
otherwise VFR prevails.

AIRMET Sierra is in effect for mtn obsc for portions of all
islands due to cld and SHRA. Conds should improve in the mid 
morning hours.

AIRMET Tango is in effect for mod turb lee of terrain. Expect this
continue for a few days.

&&

.MARINE...
High pressure north of the islands maintains fresh to strong
trades well into next week. The Small Craft Advisory (SCA) 
remains in effect for the typically windy waters around Maui 
County and the Big Island and has been expanded westward to the 
Kaiwi Channel beginning this morning. The SCA may need to be 
further expanded in areal coverage tonight as trades undergo 
modest strengthening.

A rather short period NNW swell peaked overnight in the 5 to 6 
foot range and will bring seasonable surf to N shorelines. This
swell will slowly decline and shift to the N during the weekend as 
a smaller overlapping NW swell moves through the area. Both swells
lower on Monday and fade on Tuesday. Another small NW swell is 
possible next Thursday or Friday.

A declining small, medium period S swell will maintain below
average surf along S shorelines through next week.

Surf along E shores increases this weekend as trades strengthen. 
In addition, expect some wrap of NNW swell for exposed shorelines 
through at least today. Rough trade wind swell will produce above
average surf for E shores through Tuesday before declining with 
weakening trades mid-week.

&&

.FIRE WEATHER...
Fire weather conditions will remain below critical fire thresholds
into the first half of next week. Breezy winds and minimum
humidity levels will not align to reach critical levels in any of
our fire weather zones. The subsidence inversion heights near Maui
and the Big Island will range from 9,000 to 10,000 feet elevation 
today.
