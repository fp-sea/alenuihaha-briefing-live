.SYNOPSIS...
Light to moderate trade winds are expected through the weekend. 
Brief passing showers will drift into the islands on the 
northeasterly trade winds, favoring windward and mountain areas 
during the overnight to early morning hours. A more significant 
change to our weather pattern continues to evolve for next week. 
Deep tropical moisture moving across the state will bring the 
potential for heavy rain and thunderstorms to most islands 
starting around Tuesday and persisting through at least Thursday.

&&

.DISCUSSION...
A surface trough just east of the state is visible on satellite 
imagery this afternoon, with peripheral clouds and showers 
tracking into east-facing slopes of the Big Island. This feature 
will maintain enhanced cloud cover and isolated to scattered 
showers across windward Big Island and Maui through tonight, 
before drifting eastward and pulling its deeper moisture away from
the state.

Across the broader region, the surface pressure gradient remains 
relaxed as a broad low to the north splits two highs, one anchored
to its east and the other to its west. Consequently, light to 
moderate trades will persist through Monday, allowing a hybrid 
trade wind and localized land/sea breeze pattern to take hold. A 
relatively dry air mass will keep skies mostly sunny to partly 
cloudy over the smaller islands through this evening, with only 
isolated windward showers moving in on the northeasterly flow. 
Meanwhile, high-level cirrus will continue streaming overhead from
the west-southwest within a strong subtropical jet streak. 
Remnants of a dissipating frontal boundary trailing from the 
northern low as it dips southeastward will brush the western 
islands Saturday into Saturday night, providing a minor boost to 
the northeasterly trade wind speeds and windward showers across 
Kauai and Oahu.

Farther west, a low will attempt to organize near 15N and the
Dateline this weekend. The National Hurricane Center currently
assigns this feature a low (20 percent) chance of developing into
a tropical cyclone over the next 7 days. Regardless of tropical
development, the primary concern for Hawaii is an increasing risk
of heavy rainfall leading to flash flooding, and isolated
thunderstorms next week. This developing low and a large 
convergent band of deep tropical moisture to its east are expected
to get swept up by a passing mid- to upper-level trough and drawn
across the island chain. Global models continue to depict 
precipitable water (PW) values exceeding 2.5 inches over portions 
of the state late Tuesday through Wednesday, aligning with the 
strongest upper- level jet support for ascent. However, 
significant forecast uncertainty remains regarding exact timing, 
rainfall amounts, and which locations will experience the heaviest
totals. Current guidance leans toward heavy rain arriving as 
early as Tuesday and potentially lingering through Thursday. While
large-scale winds are not’t expected to be hazardous, gusty winds
may be possible within these heavy showers or in any 
thunderstorms that may develop.

.AVIATION...
A strong jet stream just to our north is forecast to shift 
southward and could produce severe turbulence in and close to 
SIGMET Romeo. Light to moderate turbulence is possible around 
Kauai and Oahu between 24 and 32,000 feet. 

Light trade winds will continue across most of the terminals where
VFR conditions will prevail. PHTO will likely see passing showers
later today and overnight which could produce brief MVFR ceilings
and visibilities. 

No AIRMETs in effect. 

&&

.MARINE...
A surface low centered about 1000 nm north of islands will move 
to the southeast through Saturday and will stall roughly 800 to
900 nm northeast of Oahu late Saturday through Monday, keeping 
the subtropical ridge just north of the area rather weak. Trade 
winds will be at gentle to moderate strength during much of this 
time, though a brief period of fresh northeast winds may accompany
a shallow band of showers moving over Kauai and Oahu late Saturday.
An area of low pressure is expected to move over the islands from
the southwest Tuesday and Wednesday, bringing variable winds and 
the potential for heavy showers and thunderstorms.

Small surf will persist along north shores through early 
Saturday. A moderate, medium-period north-northwest swell will 
build late Saturday and produce surf around seasonal average 
during its peak on Sunday. As this swell declines on Monday, a 
long-period northwest swell will arrive. This swell will generate 
moderate surf along north- and west-facing shores through 
Wednesday. A small north- northeast swell will be possible during 
the second half of next week.

Small background level surf will prevail along south-facing 
shores into early Saturday. A series of small overlapping south- 
southwest swells will push surf back to around seasonal average 
late Saturday into early next week. A larger south-southwest swell
is possible Thursday and next Friday.

Along east-facing shores, wind swell energy will be low through 
much of the week, but a small medium-period northeast swell will 
maintain small surf through tonight. Surf along most east facing 
shores will be small through early next week, but areas exposed to
wrapping northerly swells will experience periods of moderate 
surf late Saturday through Monday and again during the second half
of next week.

&&

.FIRE WEATHER...
Light to moderate trade winds with mostly dry conditions over 
leeward areas will continue to produce low to moderate fire 
weather conditions through the weekend. Ample tropical moisture 
will move in from the southwest sometime beginning Monday night or
Tuesday, bringing a threat for heavy rain through at least 
Thursday. As a result, fire weather concerns will be low through 
most of next week. Inversion heights will range from 6,000 to 
8,000 feet tonight into Saturday and will be relatively weak over 
the eastern end of the state due to a weak trough just east of the
Big Island.
