Area Forecast Discussion
National Weather Service Honolulu HI
346 AM HST Sat Oct 31 2026

.SYNOPSIS...
A plume of shallow moisture lingering in a light to moderate trade
wind flow will maintain increased cloud cover through morning, 
while wet conditions persist over mainly windward Big Island. An 
approaching disturbance aloft may trigger a few heavy showers this
afternoon and tonight, mainly over Kauai and Oahu. The tail end 
of a front will move down the island chain late Sunday through 
Monday, bringing breezy northeasterly trades and drier air in its 
wake. Breezy easterly trades will bring periods of windward and 
mauka showers through the rest of the week.

&&

.DISCUSSION...
Stubborn shallow moisture continues to produce enhanced cloud
cover over the island chain with somewhat wet conditions 
lingering on windward Big Island this morning. The surface trough
that affected the state during the past few days is now over 50
miles southeast of Big Island and drifting away as light to 
moderate trade winds remain in place over the state. Deeper 
moisture pooled along this feature will continue to produce
somewhat wet conditions across windward Big Island this morning, 
with a decrease in shower activity expected this afternoon as 
moisture decreases. A lingering band of shallow moisture continues
to stretch across the remainder of the state and extends to an 
area of thunderstorms 100 to 500 miles west of Kauai. Despite high
humidity, shower activity is expected to be limited from Maui to 
Kauai this morning, but as the upper level trough generating the 
thunderstorms to the west edges closer to the islands this 
afternoon, a few heavy showers could develop on Kauai and possibly
Oahu. 

Light trade winds will produce mainly windward rainfall from Big
Island to Maui County tonight into early Sunday morning, while 
the above mentioned upper level trough passes just north of the 
state and possibly generates more shower activity near Kauai and 
Oahu. The ECMWF is suggesting higher chances of rainfall over the 
western end of the island chain, while the GFS is keeping the 
heavier showers away from the islands. As a result, confidence is 
lower than usual. 

A shallow front will break apart as it moves down the island 
chain late Sunday and Sunday night. While this feature will
deliver some rainfall to windward and north facing terrain, the
main impact will be the development of breezy and gusty
northeasterly trade winds from west to east. Deep high pressure
building behind the front will usher in a drier and more stable
air mass, and with dew points potentially dropping into the lower
60s or may even the upper 50s, this will feel more like the first
shot of winterlike conditions than just relief from the recent 
humidity. 

A breezy and stable trade wind flow will continue through at least
the end of the work week as high pressure holds north of the state.
Dew points will remain quite low on Tuesday, then gradually 
rebound closer to normal. Expect rainfall to be largely focused 
across windward slopes, with passing light rainfall leeward.

Heading into next weekend, focus returns to the tropics again.
While the details are out of agreement, the GFS and ECMWF are 
showing possible tropical cyclone development far southeast of the
state late in the week with some chance of a tropical cyclone 
being near or south the islands next weekend. The National 
Hurricane Center continues to monitor this area, maintaining a 
medium (40 percent) chance of development over the next 7 days.

.AVIATION...
A trough to the west and ridge to the east will maintain easterly
trade winds through the morning, with significant differences in 
model solutions later by mid morning. Light showers and clouds 
will focus over primarily windward and mountain areas this 
morning. The exception will be windward Big Island, where 
increased moisture will linger through the forecast period, 
keeping lower ceilings and reduced visibility in place. This 
afternoon and evening's conditions will depend on the progression 
of the trough to the west. Half of our model solutions depict this
feature bringing deeper moisture over Kauai and moving down to 
Oahu this afternoon through tonight, along with veering winds to 
the southeast. This solution would mean more showers and low 
ceilings/reduced visibility over Oahu and Kauai tonight. The other
half of our models show drier conditions with light easterly 
winds. There is also the chance for some pop up afternoon showers 
today as low level moisture associated with the trough lingers. 

AIRMET Sierra for mountain obscuration remains in effect abv 020 
for windward areas of the Big Island, Maui, and Molokai. This could
remain through the early morning before improving late morning.

&&

.MARINE...
High pressure to the north will bring moderate to fresh trades to
the region today. Northeast winds are forecast to strengthen 
behind a trough dropping south across the islands on Sunday, which
may require a Small Craft Advisory for all coastal waters. An
incoming north-northwest swell combined with strengthening trades
will push seas in excess of 10 feet for exposed waters. Moderate
to fresh northeast trades remain in place through much of the 
week as high pressure sits north of Hawaii.

The current small, short period north-northeast swell will maintain
small surf along north- and east-facing shores today. The 
forerunners from a moderate, long-period northwest swell are 
expected to arrive this afternoon/evening, with the swell building
quickly overnight. By Sunday, advisory level surf is expected for
north and west facing shores, which is expected to continue into 
Monday. The swell is may shift slightly more northerly briefly 
build further on Monday before dropping on Tuesday. Depending on 
the exact direction, a Marine Weather Statement may eventually 
need to be issued due to the potential for harbor surges at 
Kahului and Hilo during that time. Another advisory level 
northwest swell is possible Thursday into Friday.

The current moderate, medium period south swell will gradually 
decline through the weekend. Surf above seasonal average returns
to seasonal average on Sunday as the south swell energy fades. 
During the first half of next week, south shore surf will return 
to below seasonal averages. 

East-facing shores will see an increase in trade wind swell as 
northeast winds return on Sunday. Surf along east-facing shores
will increase through Monday night and conditions will be choppy
as trades sustain through much of the week.

&&

.FIRE WEATHER...
The anomalously wet conditions during the past few months and the 
rainfall from the past few days has kept the Keetch-Byram Drought
Index (KBDI) below 500 across nearly all of the state. Fire 
weather concerns will be low through early Sunday as trade winds 
remain light and humidity elevated. Breezy and much drier trade 
winds will develop by Monday, but since KBDI will remain 
suppressed, fire weather conditions will be moderate.
