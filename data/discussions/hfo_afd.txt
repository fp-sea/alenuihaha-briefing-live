National Weather Service Honolulu HI

.SYNOPSIS...
A partly cloudy weekend under light to moderate trade winds and
increasing rain chances beginning Sunday. Scattered light showers
will occasionally pass across to the southwest. The thickest 
cloud cover and highest rainfall amounts will be confined to 
windward upslope mauka. This recent post-frontal dry air mass will
begin to moisten up tomorrow through Tuesday in response to a 
developing trough west of the state pulling up higher southern- 
based moisture. 

&&

.DISCUSSION...
Early morning regional IR satellite imagery is depicting a large 
stable field of upstream celluar-shaped cumulus. These shallow
clouds are passing across the state at quite the clip as they 
get caught up within the trade wind belt. Today's precipitation 
will be very light with primarily windward and upslope mauka areas
picking up the greatest rain accumulation. Light to moderate 
easterly trades within this relatively drier air mass will remain 
with us through the weekend. The pattern will begin to change 
tomorrow as an upper trough begins to develop west of the state. 
Stronger winds and more frequent shower activity can be expected 
early next week from this evolving scenario.

A zonal upper height pattern north of the state will begin to 
undergo change within the next 24 hours as energy dives south from
the Aleutian Islands and begins to dig out a trough west of the 
state. There is fairly high confidence amongst the guidance that a
mid to upper trough will deepen from this afternoon through 
Monday morning, possibly forming a cut-off mid to upper low west 
southwest of the islands by late Sunday or early Monday. This 
scenario will translate to more moisture-rich equatorial air being
brought up and over the islands from late Sunday through the 
majority of next week. Increasing cloudiness and humidity with 
more frequent periods of rain will occur Sunday through Tuesday, 
especially across windward-facing zones. A strengthening near 1030
mb large surface high north of the state moving east will re- 
tighten the pressure gradient over the area enough to reinstate 
statewide breezy to locally strong (at elevation) trades Sunday 
evening into early Tuesday. The early week 48 to 60 hour QPF seems
manageable over many near coastal communities while overnight 
Sunday night and Monday night anchored cells within higher 
elevations may need to be monitored for potential downstream 
flooding concerns. 

Upper ridging takes control north of the state going into the 
latter part next week. This ridging will strengthen easterlies to
near Wind Advisory level criteria, especially at high elevations 
and within valleys, from Wednesday into next weekend. While winds 
will be the main story, there will still be enough low to mid 
level moisture to maintain thicker cloud cover and more frequent 
rain episodes.

.AVIATION...
Moderate trades will persist through the day. Scattered light
showers will focus mainly along windward exposed coasts and 
mountain slopes with associated brief periods of MVFR conditions. 
Elsewhere, along leeward areas, partly cloudy skies with VFR 
conditions should prevail. By tonight, low clouds and showers will
once again fill back in along windward locations.

No AIRMETs are currently in effect.


&&

.MARINE...
High pressure will become firmly established to the northwest of 
the state today, producing northeast winds in the moderate to 
locally fresh range through Sunday. The combination of fading 
moderate north-northwest and north-northeast swells is keeping 
seas above 10 ft this morning, primarily for coastal windward 
waters. As a result, the Small Craft Advisory has been extended 
through this evening. By Sunday evening, trade winds increase to 
locally strong as the high to the northwest moves closer to the 
islands and a broad surface trough forms to the west of the 
islands. This trough will also cause the trade winds to veer 
further to the east. Around this same time, an upper level trough 
could move over the western half of the state, increasing showers 
and introducing the possibility of isolated thunderstorms for 
western waters. On Tuesday, models indicate trade winds decreasing
as a front passes far to the north and weakens the high. This 
could then be followed by restrengthening for the latter half of 
the week as the high re-intensifies. 

A moderate north-northwest swell has continued to fade overnight,
and although surf may be borderline advisory level for some 
north-facing shores early this morning, it is forecast to decline 
through the day and thus the High Surf Advisory has been allowed 
to expire. The swell will fade further through the weekend, and 
surf along north and west- facing shores will continue to decline 
through Sunday. A small to moderate northwest swell arrives late 
Sunday and peaks on Monday, providing a moderate bump to surf, 
then fades through mid-week. A larger long-period northwest swell 
follows this, arriving Wednesday and peaking likely at advisory 
levels Thursday into Friday.

Surf heights along east-facing shores will remain elevated as a
result of a medium-period northeast swell today. This swell will
fade gradually through early next week, with surf declining 
slightly through the weekend. More typical, short-period trade 
wind swell will return by Monday. Surf along south-facing shores 
should see a small bump with a long-period swell arriving and 
building through the day today, then gradually declining through 
Monday. Background small to flat levels return next week.

.FIRE WEATHER...
Primarily dry conditions with periods of isolated showers will 
continue through the day. This will warrant low to moderate fire 
weather concerns. Very dry air aloft will produce a strong 
inversion base that will range from 6,000 to 7,000 feet. Winds 
will remain at moderate speeds this weekend before strengthening 
to locally breezy speeds Monday. Relative humidity levels and 
shower activity will increase by the early next week and this will
maintain lower fire weather concerns.
