National Weather Service Honolulu HI
357 AM HST Sat Nov 21 2026

.SYNOPSIS...

Breezy trade winds gradually trend down into early next week. A 
band of low level clouds and showers will sag south along the 
eastern end of the state focusing showers along windward and 
mauka areas. Upper level ridging will nose in from the west and, 
along with a drier air mass settling in over the Central Pacific, 
assist in producing a few drier days early next week. The trade 
winds will gradually ease through midweek before a cold front 
moves through on Thanksgiving day, bringing gusty northerly winds 
and increasing chance of rain. Trade will briefly return end of
next week before another front approached the state next weekend.


&&

.DISCUSSION...

Latest surface analysis shows a high pressure northeast of the 
state that will help drive breezy trade winds through this 
morning before gradually trending down this afternoon. Embedded 
within this trade wind flow is a band of low level clouds and 
showers that will focus primarily along windward and mauka areas, 
with some spillover expected into interior and leeward areas due 
to the stronger trade wind flow and slightly elevated inversion 
levels. Brief moderate to locally heavy showers will be possible 
as an upper level trough moves south over the state today, otherwise
anticipate mostly isolated to moderate showers.

Remainder of the forecast remains on track. The high will 
gradually weaken and move east- southeast today into early next 
week with weak ridging settling just north of the state on Monday,
then sliding over the state by Tuesday night. In response, the 
trades will gradually weaken. At the same time, a drier airmass 
will move over the region and upper level ridging will build in 
from the west, leading to a somewhat drier, weakening trade wind 
pattern. However, there will be enough low- level moisture to 
support limited windward and mauka showers. Wednesday is looking 
like a transition day where the light winds will shift out of the 
west across the western end of the state and strengthen in advance
of an approaching cold front.

Latest guidance continues to indicate a cold front moving down 
the island chain on Wednesday evening through Thanksgiving Day. 
GFS brings the front 6 to 12 hours faster over the western half of
the state Wednesday night than the ECMWF. Upper level support for
heavy showers and thunderstorms remains well north and east of 
the state and latest EC and GFS model runs are trending down on 
the probability of precipitation. As of now, the flood threat 
remains minimal but confidence is low due to variability of 
magnitude and location of the upper level support between the 
model runs. The primary impact at this time will be from gusty 
northerly winds following behind the front. A new surface high 
pressure will build north of the state Friday allowing easterly 
trades to return at more normal speeds briefly before another 
front approaches the state from the northwest next weekend.

.AVIATION...

Breezy trades will usher in isolated to scattered showers enhanced
by an upper trough that will continue to build over Kauai and 
shift south towards the eastern end of the island chain through 
this weekend. VFR conditions will prevail with brief periods of 
MVFR over windward and mauka areas.

AIRMET Tango is posted for leeward sections of all islands below 
7000 feet due to the breezy trade winds, which is expected to 
continue over the next couple of days.

No other AIRMETS are currently in effect.


&&

.MARINE...

A high will move southeast and weaken through Sunday, causing 
trades to gradually ease and the SCA to eventually be scaled back 
to the typically windy waters around Big Island and Maui. As the 
subtropical ridge is displaced southward early next week, winds 
will decrease further, and the SCA will likely be dropped sometime
Monday. The ridge will be pushed near or over the islands on 
Tuesday, and an approaching front will likely produce increasing 
southwest winds late Wednesday.

Small surf will prevail along north- and west-facing shores for 
the next few days followed by much larger swells next week. A 
tiny north swell will be overlapped by a small north-northwest 
that will peak on Sunday and decline on Monday. A large northwest
swell will build on Tuesday and will push surf along north- and 
west-facing shores around High Surf Advisory thresholds into 
Wednesday. An extra large northwest swell is possible on 
Thanksgiving Day, though a front likely moving down the island 
chain at the same time could bring fresh northerly winds.

Strong trade winds will maintain rough surf above seasonal 
averages along east-facing shores today. As trades decline, east 
shore surf will fall below seasonal averages Sunday and will be 
very small through Wednesday. 


&&

.FIRE WEATHER...

Breezy trades will gradually ease today through midweek next 
week. A moderate fire weather threat is expected today as winds
remain breezy, and relative humidities hover near thresholds. A 
drier airmass will settle in early next week bringing the relative
humidities down, but at the same time, wind speeds will also be 
on the decline. These conditions will make a low to moderate 
threat early next week. Temperature inversion heights will likely 
remain between 7,000 to 9,000 feet through tonight.
