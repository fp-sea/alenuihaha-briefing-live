.SYNOPSIS...
A high pressure ridge to the north of the state will remain locked
in place for the foreseeable future keeping Tropical Storm Pena
moving in a westerly direction, passing well south of the 
Hawaiian Islands. Expect a transition to breezy trade winds today
with drier trends through the weekend, lasting into the first half
of next week. The next best chance for increasing statewide shower
activity arrives from next week Thursday to Friday as an unsettled
cloud band associated with a dissipated cold front moves in from 
the north.

&&

.DISCUSSION...
The satellite imagery this morning shows more stable stratocumulus
clouds east of the Hawaiian Islands riding in on the trade winds.
These low level cloud features are more stable indicating stronger
subsidence aloft and lower trade wind temperature inversion
heights. This upstream stability validates our drying trends
though Sunday as the upper low that was anchored west of the state
dissipates and the deep tropical moisture surge associated with
the passing easterly wave wanes. 

Tropical Storm Pena remains roughly 525 miles south-southeast of 
South Point on the Big Island of Hawaii. Pena will continue on a
east to west track under the steering influence of the lingering 
high pressure ridge north of the state keeping the tropical storm
impacts well south of the island chain. Easterly winds may 
approach wind advisory thresholds in some terrain favored areas. 
However, at this time we are not expecting to issue wind 
advisories as Tropical Storm Pena passes south of the state.

Large scale atmospheric downward vertical motions (subsidence)
over the next several days will cause the trade wind temperature
inversion heights to fall, lowering cloud heights, and limiting
shower activity from later today through Wednesday. Temperature
inversion heights will range from 6,000 to 7,000 feet elevation
during this time period, high enough for brief passing showers
mainly along windward mountain ranges, favoring the overnight to 
early morning hours. Rainfall amounts over Maui and the Big Island
will trend lower as compared to the past several days.

The next best chance for increasing statewide shower activity 
arrives from next week Thursday to Friday as an unsettled cloud 
band associated with a dissipated cold front moves in from the 
north. Expect northern and eastern slopes of all islands to see
additional beneficial rainfall as breezy easterly trade winds 
lift these unsettled clouds up island mountain slopes. 

&&

.AVIATION...
Moderate to locally breezy trade winds will persist through the 
period. Pockets of higher moisture riding in on the trades early
this morning will guarantee occasional lower decks falling to low
VFR or MVFR flight categories along with more frequent windward 
showers especially at Hilo (PHTO). Better organized rain clusters
passing over windward terminals this morning will produce more 
frequent periods of MVFR in both lowered ceiling and visibility. 
Conditions will begin to slowly improve this afternoon as drier 
air moves in from the east.

AIRMET Sierra is in effect for mountain obscuration across
windward Big Island, Maui, Molokai and Oahu. Sierra will likely 
will allowed to expire by noon with lifting decks to VFR levels. 

AIRMET Tango remains in effect for moderate turbulence within the
lee of higher terrain.

&&

.MARINE...
Fresh to strong trade winds will prevail through through the
remainder of this weekend as high pressure dominates north of the
state and Tropical Storm Pena moves westward, several hundred 
miles south of the state. The Small Craft Advisory (SCA) has been 
extended through Sunday night for most Hawaiian Waters due to a 
combination of winds and elevated seas. The Big Island windward 
waters were dropped due to wind speed guidence falling just below
SCA criteria. Winds are expected to ease slightly late Monday and
Tuesday as the ridge to our north erodes and Pena moves away to 
the southwest. The SCA will likely be trimmed back to the typical 
windy waters around Big Island and Maui at that time. Trade winds 
are expected to strengthen again on Wednesday.

A small to moderate, medium period, northwest swell will continue
to affect north and west facing shores today. A larger 
reinforcing medium- period swell out of the north- northwest (325
to 340 degree) will arrive by late this evening and fill in 
tonight. Surf could reach advisory thresholds during the peak of 
the swell late tonight into Sunday morning. Another large 
northwest to north- northwest swell is set to arrive late 
Wednesday into Thursday.

A small south-southwest swell should produce surf slightly above 
seasonal average today. A tiny pulse from the south may maintain 
south shore surf near seasonal average Sunday and Monday, followed
by background level surf through the remainder of the week.

Rough surf along east-facing shores will hold at or above 
seasonal average through this weekend. Surf will decrease slightly
by early next week as trades begin to ease. 

&&

.FIRE WEATHER...
A high pressure ridge to the north and Tropical Storm Pena passing
south of the islands will keep breezy trade winds in the forecast
with decreasing humidity levels. Fire weather conditions will 
remain below critical thresholds into next week. Temperature 
inversion heights today near Maui and the Big Island will drop
into the 6,500 to 7,500 foot elevation range.
