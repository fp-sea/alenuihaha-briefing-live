.SYNOPSIS...
Surface high pressure northwest of the Hawaiian Islands will 
slowly slide east over the next several days. Passing showers are 
possible over windward and mauka areas during this time period, 
mainly during the overnight to early morning hours. The next best 
chance for statewide rainfall should arrive Tuesday and Wednesday 
as increased low level moisture arrives with an old cold front 
drifting into the region.

&&

.DISCUSSION...
Radar indicates scattered light showers across windward and mauka
areas, and the open waters early this morning. Some continued
uptick in coverage is expected this morning, but showers will
weaken outside of windward areas this afternoon. 

Mid level ridging will remain centered overhead through the middle
of next week. Surface high pressure northwest of the islands today
will slowly slide east to our northeast Sunday night. An upper 
level low will approach from the northwest Tuesday. In addition, a 
band of low level moisture associated with an old frontal 
boundary will move across the state from the east and northeast. 
These features will lead to an increased chance for showers
midweek. Maintained very high PoPs for windward and mauka
locations, but even leeward areas may see a few showers at times. 

The upper low will dissipate as it finally reaches Hawaii around
Thursday. Weak, broad ridging may build somewhat to our west late
week and next weekend. This flow pattern may allow for more old 
fronts to pass through the state, but the timing of these is of 
low confidence at this range. 

&&

.AVIATION...
Trade winds will continue with brief periods of passing showers
over windward and mountain areas favoring the overnight to early
morning hours. Improving weather trends during the daylight hours.

AIRMET Tango remains in effect for low level moderate turbulence 
over and downwind of island mountains. 

&&

.MARINE...
High pressure north of Hawaii will drive fresh to locally strong
trade winds today. A new high pressure system building north of
the islands will cause trade winds to strengthen back to strong
levels for most waters by tonight. Therefore, the Small Craft 
Advisory (SCA) remains in effect for the typical windy waters near
Maui County and the Big Island today, with an expansion to cover 
all coastal waters tonight. Winds will diminish somewhat on Sunday
as the high shifts quickly to the northeast, but a new north swell
will push seas above 10 feet for exposed waters which will prompt
an extension of the SCA for at least some zones beyond just the 
typically windy ones (likely all windward zones). Next week will 
see continuing fresh to strong trades with some minor fluctuations
each day. 

A moderate, medium-period north-northwest swell will continue to 
decline today. A new small, long-period north-northwest swell will 
help support small surf through the day before the arrival of a more 
significant north swell late Saturday night. This moderate, long-
period north swell will push surf to near advisory levels on Sunday 
for north and west-facing shores. This swell, at around 350
degrees, looks to peak around 6 feet, 15 seconds Sunday afternoon
and evening, which may lead to surges into north-facing harbors. 
Surf to at least moderate levels, along with harbor surge 
potential, will last into Monday before the swell really begins to
fade on Tuesday. A small to moderate northwest swell is then 
possible by next weekend.

Surf along east-facing shores will remain rough and choppy due to
the fresh trade winds and increase tonight into Sunday with
strengthening trades. South shore surf will stay tiny to small for 
the next few days, then a small long-period swell could boost surf 
heights towards the end of next week.

&&

.FIRE WEATHER...
Fire weather conditions will remain below critical, limited by 
wind speeds for most areas and by higher minimum humidity levels 
for the higher elevation zones that reach wind thresholds. Wind 
speeds will increase into the moderate to breezy levels later 
today, lasting into early next week. Temperature inversion heights
near Maui and the Big Island will range from 6,500 to 7,500 feet 
elevation today.
