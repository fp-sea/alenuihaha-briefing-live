The atmosphere near the Big Island is expected to remain rather moist through Sunday. As a result, periods of fog and high humidity are still likely until then. The inversion is expected to strengthen slowly through Sunday, but will remain rather weak. While this could help reduce the risk of fog and precipitation, humidity will decrease only slowly and fog might still pop up at times. Drier conditions are expected from late Sunday and Monday, but more moisture could enter the area starting on Tuesday. As the clouds in the area break down, a partially clear sky is expected for tonight. Nevertheless, more mid/high clouds will fill in from the NW on Saturday, contributing to another round of scattered/patchy cloud cover for Saturday night and into early Sunday evening. These clouds will rapidly break down and shift easthward during the latter part of the evening, leaving clear skies for part of Sunday night and for Monday night before mid-level clouds might fill the area from the south on Tuesday. Precipitable water will exceed the 4 mm threshold through Sunday evening. It is expected to suddenly drop and range between 1 and 2 on Sunday night and between 1 and 1.5 on Monday night. It will start increasing again on Tuesday night. Observing conditions will remain rather poor and variable tonight and Saturday night as moisture lingers in the area and winds gradually strengthen through tonight and Saturday. Boundary layer turbulence will in fact increase through Sunday. In addition, there is a high probability that free atmospheric turbulence will increase on Sunday night and into early Monday evening, further delaying improvement during that time. There is a small to moderate possibility that seeing will begin to improve as the dynamic turbulence subsides on Monday night, before conditions might degrade again on Tuesday. The current weather scenario is dominated by a deep trough now shifting eastward, an upper-level low to the west of the islands’ chain, and another upper-level trough approaching from the NNW. Under these conditions, moisture is expected to linger in the area. As the troughs shift eastward, moisture is first expected to decrease slowly through today, easing the chances for fog and lingering moisture at the summit tonight. However, as the second trough pushes forward, another bout of moisture and clouds will enter the area on Saturday and early Sunday, increasing the chances for fog and moisture again while also strengthening surface winds. Observing conditions will therefore be less than optimal until then. Dry and stable conditions will return to the summit once the inversion reaches full strength near 7,000 ft and the residual moisture is pushed southward by late Sunday morning. Northerly flow along a building ridge will then start promoting a more stable atmosphere on Monday. Models currently project worsening conditions on Tuesday. This projection will be revisited with the next forecast updates.
