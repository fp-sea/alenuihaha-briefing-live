2 PM HST Friday 09 October (0000 UTC Saturday 10 October) 2026

The trade wind inversion is expected to recover throughout today and this evening, allowing dry, stable conditions to return to the summit through at least Saturday night. However, more moisture is projected to move in from the east, and the inversion is expected to weaken and fluctuate near the summit for the rest of the forecast period. Fog and high humidity are very likely from Sunday onward. Afternoon clouds will be minimal on Saturday but will increase on Sunday, and especially early next week. The bulk of the mid- and low-level clouds that affected the area are shifting westward and dissipating, so clear skies are expected tomorrow and for most of Sunday. However, patches of mid- to high-level clouds will move in from the east and spread across the northern skies starting late Sunday night. Together with lingering moisture and fog at the summit level, these clouds may result in cloudy skies for the remainder of the forecast period. Precipitable water is expected to exceed 4 mm tonight, drop to near 2 mm on Saturday night, and begin rising again on Sunday (3-4 mm). It will remain well above the 4 mm threshold from Monday onward. Seeing is expected to improve this evening as the atmosphere dries and stabilizes. However, another round of boundary layer turbulence will degrade seeing again for the rest of the weekend. Surface winds are expected to strengthen from late tonight through Sunday, resulting in poor ground layer conditions. Additional moisture and instability early next week may also keep seeing from improving. The current weather pattern is dominated by a broad, strong upper-level low to the NNW of the state, which is slowly dissipating and lifting northward. This low has drawn a stream of deep tropical moisture toward the islands, increasing moisture in the air masses above the summit. As that stream shifts just west of the Big Island today, the air masses will dry out through tonight. Subsidence associated with a deep ridge to the NE will move into the area by sunrise Saturday, allowing the inversion to re-establish near 6-7 thousand feet and ensuring dry, stable conditions for Saturday night.However, a tight mid-level low is expected to develop east of the Big Island and drift westward as the weekend progresses. A sharp wind gradient along its western flank will move overhead ahead of the system, increasing winds near summit level and contributing to another round of boundary layer turbulence over much of the weekend. There is a chance that moisture and instability associated with this low will wrap along the northern skies during the weekend and possibly sag southward early next week. This would erode the inversion again and increase the risk of fog and high humidity at the summit from early Monday through the rest of the forecast period.
